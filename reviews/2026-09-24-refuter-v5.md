**Verdict: `contradiction`.**

- **v3.5 answers the fifth round in substance.** Every derivation of T1–T15 fails against the new text. The whole-version rule, batched confirmation, the first version on the built-in default, the binding review of standing answers, the default for agent installs, the relaxation rules and the pairing invariants for the app are sound as designs (§3).
- **The new confirmation machinery leaks, and one leak reaches the core invariant.**

  - The per-machine default of pending item 14 contradicts §7.1 rule 6 and leaves shared elements under several processes (U9).
  - The workstation and app paths sign a digest that the node computed, so T6's attack survives in another form (U10). The first registration of a workstation key is unprotected (U11).

## 2. Findings

### Blocking

#### U1. From CM2 an agent can sign `board.confirm`, because its strength is whatever the project asks for delegations

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** blocking
- **Claim:**
  - spec:197: "**From CM2**, a confirmation is a signed board act (`board.confirm`, waggle A1 §19.1) by a confirmer, at the assurance and user verification the project requires for `delegations` (§4.2), verified against the roster at the parent revision of the confirmed head. Every machine verifies it and runs the version it confirms".
  - waggle:464: `board.confirm` carries "the project's requirements for `delegations`".
  - spec:161: before CM2 a confirmation is verified only where its key was registered, and "from CM2 it is also a signed board act that every machine verifies".
  - spec:22: "So no session can edit its own authority."
- **Conflicts with:**
  - **No policy, no requirement.** spec:288: `delegations` is "covered whenever the project sets a signature policy". spec:290-291: the `min_assurance` and `user_verified` defaults exist only "whenever a project sets a signature policy". A project without `policy.signatures` therefore requires nothing for `delegations`, and so nothing for `board.confirm`. The scrum project, a PC1 demo, sets none (scrum-project:4).
  - **Agents can sign.** spec:290: "an agent running as the operator's OS user can obtain `software` signatures from `ssh-agent` freely". spec:291: "`hardware-bound` signatures are available to an agent whenever the key does not demand presence".
  - **The schema cannot express a stricter rule.** It has no category for confirmations, and it accepts `min_assurance: "software"` with `user_verified: "if-supported"` (probes, §1).
  - **The requirements are read from the head.** waggle:621: the requirements "come from the project's process configuration at its pinned revision", and spec:183 defines that revision as the head (U3), which is the version being confirmed.
  - **§2.3 binds the signer, not the verifier.** §2.3 asks every confirmation key to need the person for every use (spec:146-159). A verifier on another machine applies spec:197 instead, and cannot see from an SSHSIG signature whether a Secure Enclave key demanded presence (waggle:183).
  - **The operator's decision.** ops:177: authority takes effect "после аутентификации человека".
- **Derivation:**
  1. **Scrum, after CM2.** `policy.confirmers` is absent, so the confirmer is the creator, `op:alice@acme`. Her roster line is an ordinary key in her `ssh-agent`, like `op:bob@acme` at waggle:68.
  2. An orchestrator session on Alice's machine commits a looser workflow. It then signs a `board.confirm` for the head through the agent socket, in the namespace `approve.board.v1@waggle`.
  3. Every machine verifies it: the signer is the creator, present in the roster, and the project requires neither assurance nor user verification. Every machine runs the change, and no person acted.
  4. **Cafe, after CM2.** The project asks for `hardware-bound` keys and `user_verified: required` (cafe-project:20-24).
     - The agent's commit C1 also lowers `policy.signatures` to `software` and `if-supported`.
     - Read at "its pinned revision", the head (waggle:621; spec:183), that weaker requirement admits a software key.
     - With U2's roster line for a key it holds, the agent confirms without using Alice's machine at all.
  5. **Before CM2 the same projects are safe.** A machine accepts only its own registered key, and that key needs the person for every use (spec:139, :148). CM2 weakens the boundary it was meant to extend to every machine.
- **Resolution:**
  - Give `board.confirm` a floor that no project setting lowers: `user_verified: required` (a FIDO2 signature with the user-verified flag, or an enrolled companion key) and at least `hardware-bound` assurance (`attested` for the companion). A project may only raise it. State this in spec §2.4 and in A1 §19.1 and §19.5, and drop "the project's requirements for `delegations`" for this type.
  - Read the requirements, `policy.confirmers` and `people` from the version the confirmation replaces, the version in force, never from the head (U3).
  - A Secure Enclave key without attested presence then confirms only on the machine that registered it, as before CM2.

### Major

#### U2. The parent of the confirmed head already holds pending roster lines, so a batch can admit its own confirmer

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major (T3 residual)
- **Claim:**
  - spec:197 and waggle:473: `board.confirm` "is verified against the roster at the parent revision of the confirmed head, so the version being confirmed cannot admit its own confirmer".
  - spec:194: "One confirmation covers every change committed since the last confirmed version: the person confirms the head".
  - spec:199: "the kernel verifies the signature of the commit it read against the roster of that commit's parent revision, so no commit admits its own signer."
- **Conflicts with:** batching. The parent of the head is the version being confirmed minus its last commit, not the version in force.
- **Derivation:**
  1. C0 is confirmed. An agent commits C1 with a roster line `op:alice@acme … <a key it holds>`: a second key for an existing confirmer (T3's step 5) and, where the policy needs one, an enrollment record.
     - C1's own signature is checked against C0's roster and passes, because agent sessions sign commits with the operator's git key, as they do today.
  2. The agent commits C2, any change.
  3. It signs `board.confirm` for C2 with the new key. The verifier reads the roster at C2's parent, C1, which lists the key. The whole batch is confirmed, C1's roster line included, by a key that only C1 admitted.
  4. **With a weak floor (U1)** an agent does this alone. **With U1's floor** the key must show user verification, so an agent cannot. A person outside the confirmers group still can: they enroll their own FIDO2 key under a confirmer's principal and confirm as that confirmer.
  5. The commit-signature check of spec:199 cannot catch this. Its failure has no stated consequence, and a commit signed with the operator's git key shows no person.
- **Resolution:**
  - Verify every confirmation against the roster, revocation list and enrollment records of the version it replaces, never against the parent of the head. That covers `board.confirm` and, where a pre-CM2 record names a principal, that record too.
  - State what a failed commit-signature check does: the head stays pending and is reported. Also state that the check shows no person; or drop it, since confirmation is the gate.

#### U3. "The pinned revision" is still the unconfirmed head

- **Type:** internal · **Severity:** major (T4 residual)
- **Claim:**
  - spec:183: the kernel "records that revision [the head], the **pinned revision** the rest of this specification refers to".
  - spec:193: while the head is unconfirmed, "the kernel runs the last confirmed version in full".
- **Conflicts with:** the rules that read the pinned revision.
  - spec:274: a pattern "is resolved against the waggle roster at the pinned revision", and a quorum counts principals present in that roster (spec:478).
  - spec:330: a session's role alias is "checked against the alias at the pinned revision".
  - spec:332: a delegated act counts only "inside the policy's ceilings at the pinned revision".
  - spec:758: the admitted playbooks are "`extends` and `uses`, read at the pinned revision".
  - waggle:466, :621 and :656: board acts are read "against the project's process configuration at its pinned revision", and their requirements "come from" it.
- **Derivation:**
  - Read literally, every rule above applies the head.
  - A pending roster line joins a pattern group and counts toward a quorum before anyone confirms it. A pending weakening of `policy.signatures` governs the signed acts that should enforce the confirmed policy (U1). A pending ceiling change governs delegated acts.
  - spec:193 says the opposite, and an implementation has to pick one of the two.
- **Resolution:**
  - Define the pinned revision as the revision of the version in force, that is, the head as it was confirmed.
  - Call the current head the proposed version, and use it only for the digest, the report and the confirmation.
  - In waggle §19.1, §19.5 and §19.8, replace "at its pinned revision" with "of the version in force".

#### U4. `task-board.config.json` still decides what runs, from the working tree and outside the digest

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major (N1's class, through a second file)
- **Claim:**
  - spec:185: "The **resolved configuration** is everything that decides what runs and who may act". The list that follows (spec:186-190) names `Playbook.json`, the templates, the tool providers, the roster, the default branch and the confirmers.
  - spec:130: "the legacy read is the one exception to the confirmation rule of §2.4".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - **Check sets.** `task-board.config.json` defines them (spec:52, :589).
    - spec:600: "An explicitly empty list is a recorded decision to run nothing".
    - spec:1034: `task-board.config.json` is a file "the kernel reads from the working tree".
    - spec:603: the executor runs a local set "in a clean checkout of the exact tree under evaluation". So the set's definition comes from the working tree or from the tree under evaluation, never from the confirmed version.
  - **Version control.** spec:54 and spec:528: the `version_control` keys are read from `task-board.config.json` for one release.
- **Derivation:**
  1. dev-cycle gates Change Request work with `delivery.checks-passed` on `handoff` and integrates with `checks: "landing"`. An agent edits `task-board.config.json`:
     - `"handoff": { "where": "local", "commands": [] }`;
     - `landing.where` loses `"hosted"`.
     - Every guard still names its set, the digest is unchanged, and no confirmation is asked.
     - Every Change Request now reaches review with no test run.
     - The board stops waiting for the provider's required checks; branch protection still applies where the host has it (spec:606).
  2. If the executor reads the set from "the tree under evaluation", the producer does the same in its own Change Request and gates itself.
  3. A project that has not migrated `version_control` loses commit acknowledgement (`delivery.commit-acknowledged`) in the same way, for the release in which the legacy keys are read. So the legacy model read is not "the one exception" (spec:130); see also U17.
  4. **The migration window.** "Until migrated, the old keys are read as before", and "the migrated `policy.data.models` takes effect only once a person confirms it" (spec:130). If "migrated" means committed, then between the migration commit and its confirmation neither the old keys nor the new admission apply. The project then admits every model.
- **Resolution:**
  - Put the check sets that a confirmed playbook names into the resolved configuration: their `where`, `commands`, `hosted` and `by_task_class`. Read them from the board's `task-board.config.json` at the confirmed head, or, where the code lives in another repository, from that repository's default branch under the same confirmation. Never read them from the working tree or from the tree under evaluation.
  - List the legacy `version_control` read beside the model keys as a second exception for its release, or read it at the confirmed head.
  - Keep the legacy model keys in force until the migrated `policy.data.models` is confirmed.

#### U5. Role skills' `data/role.json`, and admitted locations of hand copies, change roles outside the digest

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major (T2 residual)
- **Claim:**
  - spec:766: "Installing a role skill gives a role its instructions; it runs nothing and moves nobody's authority."
  - spec:198: installs stay available before the first confirmation "because it changes what roles know, never what runs or who may act".
  - spec:765: agents install audited skills by default.
- **Conflicts with:**
  - spec:676: `data/role.json` holds "capability, default needs, definition-of-done items, skills the role needs".
  - spec:682: the project's role entry "wins over the template's role entry, which wins over `data/role.json`". So `data/role.json` supplies whatever the project and the template leave out.
  - spec:186-190: role skills are not part of the resolved configuration.
  - spec:720: an unknown source is admitted when the operator's confirmed setting "admits that location".
- **Derivation:**
  1. A project binds `"reviewer": { "skill": "security-reviewer" }` from `curator-playbook-roles` (spec:699) and states no needs. It relies on the package's `data/role.json`: `require: { not_same_publisher_as: "developer" }`, capability `reviewer`, and definition-of-done items.
  2. An orchestrator installs a newer audited release whose `data/role.json` drops the requirement and two checklist items. No template and no provider changed, so the digest is unchanged, whether or not the lock is committed. Independence and the checklist gate are gone, and no person was asked.
  3. The audit checks the package's security, not that its process defaults stay the same.
  4. **Hand copies.** Once the operator admits a location such as `.claude/skills` (spec:720), every file an agent later writes there is admitted too, audited or not. A role skill written there changes a role's defaults without changing the digest.
- **Resolution:**
  - Either put each resolved role's `data/role.json` contribution, or the role skill's package identity, into the resolved configuration.
  - Or restrict `data/role.json` to what moves no authority (metadata for the instructions, and the list of needed skills), and take capability, needs and definition-of-done items from the template or the project only.
  - Admit an unknown source by content, a package identity or a digest, never by location.
  - Correct spec:198 and spec:766.

#### U6. While a provider update waits, the executor runs the new code under the confirmed binding

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major (T2 residual)
- **Claim:**
  - spec:188: the resolved configuration includes "the package identity of every skill whose commands the resolved `tools` bind".
  - spec:766: a new version of a bound provider "waits for a person … and the kernel keeps running the confirmed version meanwhile".
  - spec:578: the executor runs the shim "after checking the shim against the Curator lock (its owner must be the bound skill)".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - A confirmed version stores data, not code.
  - Curator installs one version of a skill name per scope (spec:659).
  - The owner check compares a skill name, not a package identity.
- **Derivation:**
  1. The cafe's provider `cafe-supplier-portal` is confirmed at v1.0. An orchestrator installs audited v1.1, which is the default (spec:765).
  2. If the identity in the digest comes from the committed lock and the agent does not commit, the digest does not change at all. If it comes from the installed identity, the head is pending, and the kernel runs the stored configuration, which names v1.0.
  3. Either way, when an order enters `sending`, the executor finds a shim owned by `cafe-supplier-portal` and runs v1.1. No person confirmed that code.
- **Resolution:**
  - Define the provider identity in the digest as the identity installed in the scopes the kernel resolves from.
  - The executor runs a tool only when the installed identity equals the identity in the version in force. Otherwise the invocation fails with `error`, and the board reports the pending provider update.

#### U7. A pre-CM2 confirmation signs only a digest, so an old record re-confirms a reverted version

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:158: an app confirmation is "a signature over the confirmed digest by a key registered at pairing", recorded "like one made on the machine".
  - spec:159: the workstation path "sends back only the signature".
  - spec:193: the kernel waits "while the head's authority digest is unconfirmed".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - waggle:486: `board.confirm` binds "the head it confirms, the authority digest of that version's resolved configuration and the authority digest of the version it replaces, so it cannot be replayed for another version". The pre-CM2 forms bind none of this.
  - spec:137: for the operator's files, "restoring an entry after a narrowing needs the authentication again". The process has no such rule.
- **Derivation:**
  1. A person confirms D0. Later the person confirms D1, which revokes a stolen key, removes a delegation rule or adds a `never` rule.
  2. An agent reverts that commit. The head's content, and so its digest, is D0 again.
  3. D0 carries a valid record by the registered key. A kernel that asks whether the head's digest is confirmed gets yes and runs D0. The narrowing is undone, and no person acted.
  4. A record also names no head. If the head moves while the person reads the report, nothing says which configuration the record confirms.
- **Resolution:**
  - Every confirmation record, in every form, binds the board, the head, its authority digest and the digest of the version it replaces.
  - The kernel accepts a record only when the replaced digest is the one in force.
  - A head whose digest equals an older confirmed digest is a new change, and it waits.

#### U8. A revocation waits for a confirmation like any other change

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major (a consequence of T3's resolution)
- **Claim:** spec:189 and spec:192: the revocation list is part of the resolved configuration, and a change to "a roster line" takes effect "only once a person has confirmed".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - waggle:85 (§3.1): "revocation lists are for emergencies … the revocation list lives in the repository and is checked on every verification".
  - spec:329: any principal of the project's `people` may revoke a delegation, "because revoking only narrows authority". spec:137: narrowing edits of the operator's files apply at once.
- **Derivation:**
  1. A manager's FIDO2 key and PIN are stolen. A teammate commits the revocation.
  2. Until a confirmer confirms it, the kernel runs the confirmed roster. The stolen key still approves and grants, and if it belongs to a confirmer, it also confirms. It can even confirm a head that omits the revocation.
  3. If the stolen key belongs to the only confirmer, the revocation can take effect only through a confirmation made on the machine.
- **Resolution:**
  - A revocation made by a person takes effect at once, as a delegation's revocation does. That means a principal of `people`, through one of the methods of §2.3, and from CM2 a signed act.
  - A revocation that anyone else commits waits with the rest. Additions to the roster keep waiting.
  - Whether a person's other narrowing acts should also take effect at once is the operator's choice (§5, D4). Examples are disabling a tool or a schedule, or applying a first version's data-policy narrowing.

#### U9. The per-machine default of item 14 contradicts §7.1 and leaves shared elements under several processes

- **Type:** internal · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:** spec:196: "Each machine runs the version its own confirmer confirmed, the board records every machine's confirmed digest, and the kernel reports machines whose confirmed digests differ. One board then has one process in force per machine."
- **Conflicts with:**
  - spec:643 (§7.1 rule 6, unchanged since v2): "teammates who resolve a different digest get a refusal naming both".
  - spec:481 and waggle:640: an approval counts only "while the configuration digest it binds is the one in force".
  - spec:548: before CM2 "a shared board runs tick on one machine only".
  - spec:198: a project with no confirmation runs the built-in default.
  - spec:706: templates resolve through the "project, hybrid, global" scopes, and the last two differ per machine.
- **Derivation:**
  1. **Rule 6.** Bob's machine has a different confirmed digest. §7.1 refuses it, while §2.4 runs its version. Both cannot hold.
  2. **Approvals.** Alice approves on her machine, binding D_A. Bob's orchestrator runs on a machine in force at D_B and never counts that approval. Each machine voids the other's approvals and grants until both confirm the same version. The divergence report describes the stall but does not end it.
  3. **Tick.** Timers, schedules and tool invocations fire only on the tick host, under its version.
     - An element another machine created in a state the tick host's version lacks has no defined timer.
     - An automatic transition that only Bob confirmed never fires.
  4. **Machines without a confirmer.** With the default confirmer, the creator, no other machine has her key registered (spec:196). Those machines run the built-in default over elements of types it does not know.
  5. **Resolution differs by machine.** A template installed in Alice's global scope but not in Bob's gives one head two digests, so each confirmer confirms something different. CM2's "runs the version it confirms" also presumes a shared store of the confirmed configuration, which the spec does not name (U20).
- **Resolution:**
  - Make single-host the default before CM2: item 14's alternative, which budgets and tick already require.
  - Or make the per-machine rule fail closed:
    - a machine whose confirmed digest differs from the latest one recorded on the board does not move shared elements of a type or state its version lacks;
    - approvals and grants count only on machines in force at the digest they bind;
    - §7.1 rule 6 says which rule applies.
  - Resolve the kernel's templates only from the project scope and its committed lock, or run on every machine the confirmed configuration stored on the board.
  - The operator's choice on item 14 should see these consequences (§5, D2).

#### U10. The signer signs a digest the node computed, so T6's attack survives

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major (T6 residual)
- **Claim:**
  - spec:159: the workstation "fetches the pending change from the node over SSH — the entries, the changes and their digest … renders it on the workstation, signs it … and sends back only the signature".
  - spec:157: the app "renders … the changes and their digest … and the signature covers exactly what it rendered". spec:158: the record is "a signature over the confirmed digest".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - spec:159 itself treats processes on the node as hostile: "a forwarded agent lets any process of the operator's account on the node request signatures".
  - The SSH command runs in the operator's account on the node. Agents there can edit the shell's start-up files, and `task-board` is a user-built binary linked into `~/.local/bin` (skill-project-management `CLAUDE.md`, Setup).
- **Derivation:**
  1. An agent on a headless node commits a change that adds an automatic exit from `approval`.
  2. It arranges for the node-side command to return a report of a harmless change, together with the true digest of its own head.
  3. The workstation renders the harmless report and signs the digest. Nothing lets it compute the digest from what it rendered.
  4. The node verifies a valid signature over its head's digest and runs the change. A person acted, but not on what they saw. This is the outcome T6 described, over another transport.
- **Resolution:**
  - The signer receives the complete resolved configuration of the head in canonical form and computes the authority digest itself.
  - It computes the report against the configuration it confirmed last, which it keeps, or re-fetches and checks against the digest it signed then.
  - It signs only a digest it computed. The same holds for the app and for widening entries of the operator's files.
  - The kernel runs only a configuration that hashes to the signed digest.

#### U11. The first registration of a workstation key, and the Secure Enclave option, let a node accept a key that does not need a person

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major (T9 residual)
- **Claim:**
  - spec:159: the key "whose every use needs user verification (a FIDO2 key with user verification, or a Secure Enclave key with user presence)" is "registered once through the same path when the node is initialized".
  - spec:155: re-initializing "a node that already has a registered key" needs a confirmation.

#### U12. Confirmers are unenforceable before CM2 and undefined for today's boards and first versions

- **Type:** assumption · **Severity:** minor
- **Claim:**
  - spec:195: "A person is a principal of the project's `people` in the group that `policy.confirmers` names; without it, the operator who created the project, whose principal the board records at creation."
  - spec:196: before CM2 a confirmation is "a signature by a key registered on the machine that verifies it".
- **Derivation:**
  - **Before CM2 the group has no effect.** A registered key names no roster principal: it is registered "once through the same confirmation" (spec:139), and nothing ties it to `people`. So the group that `policy.confirmers` names cannot be checked, and whoever registered a key on a machine is its confirmer.

#### U13. `x-` keys are left out of the digest but carry meaning in `user_verified`

#### U15. "No run writes an authority-bearing field" blocks the cafe's buyer

- **Type:** internal · **Severity:** minor (example against text)
- **Claim:** spec:384. A field is authority-bearing when a `field` guard reads it, or when "the ceilings, conditions or budgets of `policy.delegation` or of a granted delegation read" it. "No run writes such a field on any element, its own or another; the orchestrator, a person and the kernel may."
- **Conflicts with:**
  - cafe-project:27-30: the approvals rule has a ceiling on `amount`, and its description says "The orchestrator's buyer runs draft the orders".
  - cafe:47: `amount` is `required`. cafe:84: `draft` is owned by `buyer`, and "Supplier and amount are required before the order leaves draft".
- **Derivation:**
  1. The ceiling reads `amount`, so no buyer run may write it. The owner of `draft` cannot complete the field its own state requires, and an order leaves draft only when the orchestrator session or a manager writes the amount.
  2. A grant's conditions may add stricter conditions on other fields (spec:316). Granting "orders from Milk & Co only" therefore makes `supplier` unwritable by every run in the project.
  3. When a confirmed version adds a `field` guard on a field, values that runs wrote earlier start deciding routes.
- **Resolution:**
  - Choose and state one of two rules:
    - the run that owns an element's initial state may write fields that only delegation ceilings read, while the element is in that state; the value then binds approvals, and the rule's `self_approval` already accepts that;
    - or the orchestrator writes such fields, and the cafe's description says so.
  - Limit a grant's conditions to the fields its rule's ceilings read, or state that a grant makes its fields unwritable by runs.
  - State what happens to values written by runs when a confirmation makes their field authority-bearing.

#### U16. Delegation details: relaxation ceilings, mixed install rules and `board.install`

- **Type:** internal · **Severity:** minor (T10 and T12 residuals)
- **Claim:**
  - spec:311: ceilings are "`field` conditions that every delegated act must meet", and a rule that covers installs only carries none.
  - spec:860: a delegated relaxation "answers one `ask` with the role's approval scope and expiry".
- **Derivation:**
  - **Mixed rules.** A rule with `acts: ["installs", "relaxations"]` and a ceiling passes the schema (probe). An installation has no element whose field the ceiling could read, so either installs under that rule are impossible or the ceiling silently does not bind them.
  - **Relaxation ceilings.** A ceiling on a relaxation is checked on the element that asked. The exception it creates takes the role's scope, `project` or an ancestor, so it later admits runs on elements that are outside the ceiling and outside the grant's own subtree scope (spec:317).
  - **`board.install`.** waggle:463 signs it "where the project asks", but the schema has no `installs` category (the probe with `require_for: ["installs"]` is refused) and no `user_verified` default for it.
  - **Grants.** A grant's `relax` need not lie within its rule's `relax`. spec:316 asks for "stricter" only for conditions.
- **Resolution:**
  - Ceilings bind only acts that concern an element. Refuse a ceiling on a rule that mixes installs with other acts, or say that it binds only the element acts.
  - An exception created under a delegated relaxation takes the intersection of the grant's scope and the role's approval scope, and its ceilings are checked each time it applies.
  - Add an `installs` category with its default, or drop "where the project asks".
  - A grant's `relax` must lie within its rule's.

## 4. Freeze judgement

**v3.5 cannot be frozen as the PC1 target once only its minor findings are fixed.**

- U1 is blocking, and U2–U11 are major.
- **Six change text that PC1 implements:** the pinned revision (U3), the inputs of the digest (U4, and the definition of the provider identity in U6), confirmation records (U7), the per-machine rule (U9), and the workstation path (U10, U11).
- **The others change later milestones:** U1, U2 and U8 change waggle A1 and PC3, and U5 changes PC2. PC1 should still compute the digest and write its records in their final form from the start.
- **No person is needed** for any of them. U9 bears on item 14, and the wider form of U8 is a choice for the operator (D4).
- **The fixes are local:**
  - §2.3: records, a digest the signer computes itself, and registration;
  - §2.4: the pinned revision, the digest's inputs, the confirmers and the per-machine rule;
  - §2.1, §6.11 and §6.12: the check sets, and the executor's identity check;
  - §8.1 and §8.6: role defaults and admitted locations;
  - §7.1 rule 6, and §14 points 13–15;
  - waggle §3.1, §19.1 and §19.5.
- After a v3.6 with these fixes, a check limited to those places, together with the operator's answers to items 13 and 14, suffices. The minor findings can be fixed in the same pass.

**What is sound and need not be reopened:**

- the whole-version rule and batched confirmation, as designs;
- the first version on the built-in default;
- the binding review of standing answers;
- the default for agent installs, and the relaxation rules;
- the app's pairing invariants;
- the delegation model, the delivery invariants, the executor of check sets and model admission;
- everything §3 lists as holding.
