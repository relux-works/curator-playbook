## 2. Findings

### Major

#### S1. A delegation to a role can be exercised by any operator's orchestrator session

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:214: "a run or session of an orchestrator role may act for a person within a delegation that person granted";
  - spec:262: the delegate is named by `to`, "roles with capability `orchestrator`";
  - spec:264: the grant records "the grantor principal and the delegate role";
  - spec:282: "Either the session key's certificate is certified by the grantor's key with the delegation's constraints, or the act references the delegation record the grantor signed";
  - waggle:636: the board verifies "the delegate signature, whose principal belongs to the delegated role".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - waggle:58: each orchestrator session key is "certified by the operator's key", that is, by the operator who started it. waggle:613: every orchestrator session line admits `delegate.board.v1@waggle`.
  - Exceptions and delegated relaxations reach the grantor's "own runs only" (spec:778, :781). waggle:466: "an exception never reaches another operator's runs".
  - The delegation requirement: a person delegates *their* signing right (ops:161, "человек делегирует оркестратору право подписи в рамках ограничений"). A delegation must not widen what anyone other than the grantor may do.
- **Derivation:**
  - cafe-project:8-10 has managers alice and bob and accountant carol. alice grants manager approvals of up to 500 EUR to `cafe-orchestrator` (spec:272-275).
  - carol could not approve as a manager herself: waggle:465 accepts `board.approve` only from the manager group. But carol can start her own `cafe-orchestrator` session, whose key her own key certifies. In the reference form nothing ties that session to alice: the act names alice's grant in `on_behalf_of`, the session belongs to the delegated role, and the act fits the grant. The board therefore counts it as alice's approval.
  - With a manager's transitions grant under the rule at cafe-project:30-32, carol's session can send the order too. Before signatures (PC1), any session of the role on any machine acts under the grant.
  - "Belongs to the delegated role" is also undefined. Nothing maps an `orch:` principal to a playbook role alias: `people` maps only `op:` principals (spec:224), and the waggle-role certificate extension (waggle:88) is not said to carry the alias.
- **Resolution:**
  - Bind every delegation to the grantor's own sessions, in both forms: the delegate session's certificate must be issued by the grantor's key, or the grant names the session principal.
  - In PC1, a session the host marks as started by the grantor, stated as a guardrail.
  - Define how a session principal is bound to a playbook role, for example as the certificate extension's value checked against the role alias at the pinned revision.

#### S2. Approvals and delegation ceilings still do not bind the values they depend on

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major (R7 reopened)
- **Claim:**
  - spec:424: "Every approval, signed or not, records the values of the fields that the guards of the approved transition read … An order approved at 620 is not sent at 6,200".
  - spec:329: "A field that a `field` guard of the element's workflow reads decides routes, so a run may not write it on the element it was spawned for; the orchestrator, a person and the kernel may."
  - spec:832: the threshold exception "is sound at run time only because fields a guard reads are authority-bearing (§5.2), approvals bind the values they approved (§6.4)…".
  - spec:279: a delegated act counts "only inside the delegation's acts, scope, conditions, budgets and expiry and inside the policy's ceilings".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - cafe:103-109: `approval → sending` carries only `{ "guard": "approvals", "role": "manager", "min": 1 }`. No cafe-ops workflow has a `field` guard; the cafe's only `field` conditions are the delegation ceilings (cafe-project:28, :31).
  - waggle:478: the signed payload binds "the element fields shown to the approver, which include at least every field that the guards of the act's transition read". waggle:480 and :621: the board compares "the bound values", counting an approval "only while the element's fields still hold the values it binds". The examples at waggle:501 and :662 bind `supplier` and `amount`.
  - R7's resolution (reviews v2:244): "Bind every approval, signed or not, to the fields it depends on".
- **Derivation:**
  1. **The cafe binds nothing.** A manager approves "Milk & Co, 620 EUR". A run then changes the supplier and the amount: neither field is guard-read, so any run may write them. The approval still counts, so §6.4's own example fails on the process it describes. A signed approval of the same order binds `supplier` and `amount` under A1. The same act is valid unsigned and void signed, and the two documents disagree on what the board compares.
  2. **The ceilings do not protect their fields.** Ceilings (cafe-project:28, :31), grant conditions and budgets (spec:273-274) read `amount`. They are not "a `field` guard of the element's workflow", so `amount` is not authority-bearing in the cafe and approvals do not bind it. spec:279 does not say whether conditions are checked when the delegated act is recorded or when a guard later counts it.
     - Checked at the act: the orchestrator approves 450 under a 500 grant, and the budget counts 450. The amount then becomes 900, and the orchestrator sends the order under a manager's transitions grant, which the project's rule caps at 1,000 (cafe-project:30-32). 900 EUR is spent on an approval capped at 500, and the budget counted 450.
     - Checked when the guard counts: queued approvals switch in and out of validity as fields change, and the budget no longer matches what it counted.
  3. **The protection covers only the element's own run.** Runs spawned for other elements may write a guard-read field: a developer run on a sibling task, a buyer run on another order. The orchestrator, an agent, may write the field and then fire the unguarded route itself, since it fires agent-owned exits "for routing" (spec:202). §10's soundness argument therefore does not hold against agents: a model can still choose the value that decides whether a person approves.
  4. When two transitions out of one state carry `approvals` guards under different `field` guards, "the approved transition" is undefined: a `board.approve` names the role, not a transition (waggle:475).
- **Resolution:**
  - An approval, delegated or not, records every declared field of the element as shown or recorded at the act, and counts only while those values are unchanged. Spec §6.4 and waggle §19.1/§19.7 then say the same.
  - Fields read by `policy.delegation` ceilings, grant conditions and budgets are authority-bearing, like fields of `field` guards.
  - A guard-read field is written only by a person or the kernel, or at least never by the actor that fires the route it selects. Adjust the soundness sentence at spec:832 to match.
  - Conditions and ceilings are checked at the act and again when a guard counts it, against the bound values.

#### S3. Delegation budgets have no single counter and no defined accounting

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:269: the grant records "optional budgets per period: a sum of a field, or a count of acts, over a duration", for example spec:274, `{ "sum": "amount", "max": 2000, "per": "P1W" }`.
  - waggle:639: "The board counts budgets from the delegated acts it has recorded; no signature carries budget state."
- **Conflicts with:**
  - spec:491: a board shared across machines needs waggle's lease store (CM2) for tick; "Until CM2, a shared board runs tick on one machine only". Delegated acts have no such rule, and they come from sessions on any teammate's machine (S1).
  - spec:280: an act "over … a budget" is not taken, which presumes one authoritative count.
- **Derivation:**
  - **Concurrent overspend.** On a board shared across machines (git-synced, from PC1 until CM2) each kernel counts only the acts it recorded. Two sessions on two machines each see 1,800 of 2,000 used, and each approves 150: 2,100 are approved and both acts are valid. Even on one machine, the spec does not make the budget check and the record one transaction.
  - **Undefined accounting.** The spec does not say:
    - whether a budget counts the value at the act or the current value (S2);
    - whether an approval voided later (a field change, a re-entry, a digest change; spec:424-425) gives its amount back;
    - whether a grant covering `approvals` and `transitions` counts one order twice, once for its approval and once for its send;
    - whether `P1W` is a rolling window or a calendar week.
  - **No total cap.** With alice's and bob's grants of 2,000 a week each, the orchestrator may commit 4,000 and chooses on whose behalf each act is taken; `policy.delegation` offers only per-act ceilings (schema:1934).
- **Resolution:**
  - Name one owner of delegation state: the holder of the board's lease on one machine, waggle's lease store across machines. Make "check the budget and record the act" one compare-and-set.
  - Until CM2, admit delegated acts on a shared board only on the machine that holds the board's lease, as tick does.
  - Define the counted value (the bound value at the act), the refund on voiding, per-act-kind counting and the window. Consider a project-level budget in `policy.delegation`.

#### S4. Revocation and expiry treat in-flight acts inconsistently

- **Type:** internal · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:281: "Revocation is immediate and never retroactive: acts recorded under the delegation stay valid". waggle:641: the board refuses delegated acts "that arrive later; acts it accepted before stay valid". decisions:138 records the rule among the operator's decisions.
  - spec:279: a guard counts a delegated act "only inside the delegation's … expiry"; spec:280: nothing is taken "after expiry".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - spec:779: an exception that expires mid-run "lets that run finish; new runs refuse". That is a third rule for the same question.
  - ops:161: the person confirms "only what is critical"; revoking is how that person takes an authority back.
- **Derivation:**
  - A delegated approval authorizes a later transition. After alice revokes her approvals grant, an approval recorded before the revocation still satisfies the `approvals` guard of `approval → sending`. If the orchestrator holds a separate transitions grant, which cafe-project:30-32 allows, it sends the order after the revocation. A person who revokes because the orchestrator misbehaves cannot stop approvals already queued, short of editing each element.
  - Read as spec:279 words it, expiry voids the same queued approval once the guard evaluates after the expiry. A routine expiry thus voids queued approvals while a revocation, the stronger signal, keeps them. Read the other way, with expiry checked at the act, the two agree but spec:279 is wrong.
  - Before CM2, "immediate" does not hold across machines, for S3's reason: a revocation recorded on one kernel is unknown to another until the board syncs. waggle:641 concedes the gap for certificates ("short certificate lifetimes bound the exposure further").
- **Resolution:**
  - One rule for revocation and expiry: an ended delegation stops counting its unconsumed acts (approvals no transition has used), keeps consumed ones, and lets a working run finish, as §9 does for exceptions.
  - State where a revocation takes effect across machines (S3's owner).

#### S5. One delegate can meet a quorum and approve what it wrote

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:279: "It counts as one act of its grantor, so a quorum still needs distinct grantors"; spec:426 and waggle:622 say the same.
  - spec:422: "`min` above one is quorum voting and needs distinct principals".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - The purpose of `min` greater than one: several people must agree.
  - The rule the operator protected for delivery (ops:15): "правила delivery (… без самопроверки) нельзя обойти".
- **Derivation:**
  - **Quorum.** Under `{ "guard": "approvals", "role": "manager", "min": 2 }`, alice and bob each grant manager approvals to `cafe-orchestrator`. One orchestrator session records two delegated approvals, one for each grantor, and the guard passes: one model satisfies a two-person rule. Distinct grantors are not distinct deciders. A `never` rule cannot keep quorum approvals with people while single approvals of the same role are delegated, because it names only acts, roles and states (schema:2005).
  - **Approving its own work.** The root orchestrator fires agent-owned exits (spec:202) and may write any field, guard-read ones included (spec:329). In the cafe it can fill in an order, move it `draft → approval`, approve it for alice and send it for alice, with no second actor. Within the ceilings that may be intended (ops:161), but the spec neither says so nor lets a project refuse it.
  - **Delivery.** The delivery invariants are not reachable this way (§3: verdicts are not delegable). Still, spec:279's "counts it as the grantor's act" invites an implementer to evaluate invariant 2 and `not_same_publisher_as` against the grantor's provenance rather than the delegate's.
- **Resolution:**
  - By default a quorum counts at most one delegated act per delegate session, or none.
  - A delegated approval does not count when the delegate wrote a value it binds, unless the project allows it.
  - State that invariant 2 and independence read the delegate's provenance.

#### S6. P3: an agent can undo the operator's narrowing without authentication, and the headline overstates the defaults

- **Type:** decision-conflict · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:122: "The resolver keeps the last confirmed version of each file. A change that admits more than that version is a widening entry … the resolver applies the more restrictive of the current file and the confirmed version until the operator confirms. Narrowing edits apply at once."
  - spec:25: "what widens admission there takes effect only after the operator confirms it"; the heading at spec:120 and row 22 (spec:1001) say the same.
  - spec:125: "The defaults are `user` for metered billing and for standing answers and `none` for the others."
  - spec:127: "Until the commands ship, the resolver honours no widening entry".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - ops:159 (P3): option b, widening needs a person's confirmation through the OS user authentication. The question it answered (reviews v2:498) concerned metered admission and standing answers.
  - ops:52 (6a): "деньги оператора тратит только сам оператор".
  - decisions:134 first says "adding a model or a runtime … a wider time window. They take effect only after the operator confirms them", then that "by default metered billing and standing answers need the user's authentication".
- **Derivation:**
  1. **Re-widening.** At t0 the operator confirms `billing = ["subscription", "local", "metered"]`. At t1 the operator removes `metered` to stop spending: the narrowing applies at once, and the confirmed version stays t0's. At t2 an agent puts `metered` back. Against the confirmed version, t2 admits nothing new, so it is not a widening entry, and it takes effect without authentication. A standing answer the operator deleted comes back the same way. That is the case P3 exists for (spec:120: "an agent … could … admit metered billing").
  2. **Headline.** Under the defaults, a new model, a wider window or a new runtime binding takes effect at once. spec:25, spec:120, row 22 and decisions:134 state as universal a rule that holds for two of five kinds.
  3. **Before the commands ship.** spec:127 contradicts spec:125 for the `none` kinds. Read literally, until `curator runtimes` ships no runtime binding added to `runtimes.toml` is honoured, because the pre-confirmation baseline is the file-less one (spec:122).
- **Resolution:**
  - Measure widening against the effective version: the most restrictive of the confirmed version and every later narrowing. A narrowing then becomes the new baseline without authentication.
  - Scope spec:25, spec:120, row 22 and decisions:134 to kinds whose level is not `none`, or default every kind to `user`.
  - Make spec:127 read "no widening entry whose level is not `none`".
  - State that a runtime binding's billing class comes from facts, never from the binding, so `runtimes = "none"` cannot admit metered billing.

#### S7. Standing answers are permanent operator waivers, and delegated relaxations are wider still

- **Type:** decision-conflict · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:111: "An applied answer takes the role's approval scope and expiry (`needs.approval`) unless `until` sets an end date; an answer without an end is written explicitly, `until = "open"`." spec:780 says the same; row 22 (spec:1001) and decisions:134 record it as the operator's.
  - spec:781 (with spec:262): under a delegation of `relaxations`, an orchestrator approves `ask` exceptions.
  - spec:780: "A standing answer never applies when `policy.signatures` covers `relaxations`, because a file cannot show that a person acted at that moment."
- **Conflicts with:**
  - spec:967 (§17.1, decided): "relaxing a requirement permanently only in the project file, and temporarily through `ask`". See also spec:777-778 and spec:25 ("an operator may answer a *temporary* relaxation in advance").
  - ops:61-63, item 8: "Временные исключения".
  - R8's resolution (reviews v2:268), "either require `until` or say that answers are open-ended", left that choice to the operator; ops:159 does not record it.
- **Derivation:**
  - **Permanent waivers.** Without `until`, each exception expires, but the answer has no end: it applies again at every future `ask`, in every scope. With `until = "open"` the exceptions themselves never expire. Either way, one confirmed line relaxes a hard need for all of this operator's runs, indefinitely and outside `Playbook.json`. §17.1 reserves that permanent path to the project.
  - **Delegated relaxations.** A delegation of `relaxations` has an expiry but reaches further. The schema refuses `role` on a relaxations rule (probe; dependentSchemas at schema:1983), and a grant names no requirement terms. So it relaxes every hard need of every role, reviewer independence included, whereas a standing answer names its role key and terms (spec:98-100).
  - **The signature rule.** Under a signature policy that covers `relaxations`, the standing answer is refused because "a file cannot show that a person acted at that moment", while the delegated relaxation, signed by a session key (spec:282), is accepted. A delegated act does not show that a person acted at that moment either, so the stated reason does not separate the two.
- **Resolution:**
  - Require a finite `until` on every standing answer, bounded for example by the role's `needs.approval.expires` or by a maximum, and drop `open`. Alternatively, have the operator decide an amendment to §17.1.
  - Let `relaxations` rules and grants name roles and terms.
  - State the real difference between the two paths (a signed grant the board can verify, a local file it cannot), or treat them alike.
