## 2. Findings

### Blocking

#### T1. The authority boundary is a list, and one committed transition passes it

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** blocking (N1's class reopened)
- **Claim:**
  - spec:177: "The sections that grant authority are `people`, `services`, `policy.signatures`, `policy.enable`, `policy.data` (model admission included), `policy.delegation`, each role's `needs.require` and `on_unmet`, `tools`, and `extends`/`uses`. … so an orchestrator that commits and lands its own changes cannot raise its own ceilings. … A change to the other sections — entities, workflows, states and transitions, a role's skill and `prefer` — takes effect on commit".
  - spec:178 lists the workflow changes that count: one "that removes a human-owned state or gives it another owner kind, removes an `approvals` guard or a `risk` marker from an entry, loosens a `field` condition that routes to a person, or removes a delivery guard". spec:889 (the authority report) repeats the list.
  - spec:22: "so no session can edit its own authority".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - The operator's decision, ops:177 and row 26, answered on v3.3. v3.3's list read "role `needs` and `on_unmet`" and continued: "State owners and guards also decide who may move work, so the whole file follows the same rule rather than a list of keys" (v3.3:159). v3.4 drops that principle and narrows `needs` without marking either change, and row 26 and decisions:145 record the narrowed list as the operator's.
  - spec:238 (rule 1): "A transition with `on` … may leave a state of any owner kind. The kernel fires it".
  - spec:885: the guard-bypass rule covers only "non-automatic" entries (N32).
  - spec:538: on every entry into a state, the kernel evaluates `children.changed` transitions "as if it had just fired". Creation is entry into `initial` (spec:343).
- **Derivation:**
  1. **dev-cycle (a PC1 demo).** An orchestrator commits the two automatic transitions of the probe (§1). The schema accepts them, the listed-section digest is unchanged, and no enumerated change applies, so they take effect on commit.
     - Every decision the orchestrator creates is accepted at creation. Yet "only a person supersedes an accepted `decision`" (spec:240), and the refuter reads decisions as the operator's memory (§12).
     - Every escalation to the operator bounces back to development at once.
  2. **cafe.** An orchestrator commits `{ "from": "draft", "to": "sending", "on": { "after": "PT1M" } }` (cafe:84-86).
     - `supplier.send-order` is enabled by name (cafe-project:18), and "A tool runs only when the project lists it" (spec:561).
     - A minute after a buyer run drafts an order, the kernel enters `sending`, and the tool places the order.
     - No approval is recorded, and no signature is needed: the transition carries no `risk` marker, the `approvals` category has nothing to count, and `human-transitions` covers only a human actor (spec:262-267).
  3. **Formal missions.** An orchestrator commits `{ "from": "proving", "to": "accepted", "on": { "event": "children.changed" } }` (prove2me:76-99). Every attempt is then accepted without the Lean check, which defeats the example's point (spec:940) and the acceptance N7 protects (spec:553). No person is involved, so even a complete list of "authority taken from people" would not catch it.
  4. **Parts that decide who may act appear on neither list**, so they take effect on commit:
     - **Role bindings.** A human role's `who`: rebinding `manager` to a pattern group such as scrum's `operators = op:*@acme` widens who approves. Also a role's `kind`, `tool`, `process` and `capability`.
     - **Needs other than `require`.** A `fallback` of `{}` drops a hard need wherever `on_unmet: fallback` and relaxations are confirmed. A wider `needs.approval` widens every `ask` exception, and every standing answer that takes the role's scope. `degraded_state` is also missing. v3.3 covered all of `needs`.
     - **Guard parameters.** An `approvals` guard's `min` and `role`; `limit.then` redirects to a person.
     - **Structure and bindings.** `entities.<type>.workflow`; new types or workflows that reach a tool-owned state; `bind` of a process slot to another admitted playbook; `events.<e>.signed_by`.
     - **Project-only keys.** `policy.effort_floor` is the only project-only key (spec:208) outside the list.
- **Resolution:**
  - Restore v3.3's principle in a form the kernel can compute. Every part of the resolved configuration is authority-bearing, except an explicit allowlist of parts that cannot move authority:
    - descriptions, `ui` and `x-` keys;
    - a role's `skill` and `prefer`;
    - estimates, helpers, `policy.goals` and `policy.capacity`;
    - changes among agent-owned states that add no path into, and no automatic exit from, a human-, tool- or process-owned state, and no path around an `approvals` guard, a `risk` marker or a `field` route to a person.
    - A change the comparison cannot classify is authority-bearing.
  - Equivalently, put an authority projection into the digest:
    - for each state: its owner kind, and the group, tool or process it is bound to;
    - for each transition into or out of such a state: `from`, `to`, `on`, guards, `risk` and `limit.then`;
    - each `approvals` guard with its `role` and `min`;
    - the entity-to-workflow bindings and `bind`;
    - roles' `who`, `kind`, `tool`, `process` and `capability`;
    - `needs` other than `prefer`, `policy.effort_floor` and `events.*.signed_by`.
  - Extend §10's guard-bypass rule to automatic entries triggered by a timer or by `children.changed`.
  - Until the boundary is closed, correct spec:22, spec:177, row 26 and decisions:145.

### Major

#### T2. Templates and hand-copied skills change the process outside the authority digest

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:177: the digest covers "those sections in the committed version".
  - spec:740: agents install audited skills "when the project allows it in `policy.enable.skills` … or when a delegation covers it". spec:741: "Installing a skill gives a role its instructions; it runs nothing."
  - spec:277: "no delegate can widen the authority it acts under".
  - spec:697 (§8.4, unchanged): without Curator, "The resolver finds role skills and templates there by name; nothing in `Playbook.json` changes, and the machine checks (§10) warn that the skills are unaudited".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - **A template is a skill.** The orchestrator skill carries it (spec:24). It is resolved "through Curator's locks … the first scope that holds a skill whose `data/playbook.json` has that name wins" (spec:685).
  - **Names do not pin versions.** `uses` items are bare names (schema:52-57), and `extends` pins a package only in its object form.
  - **The prototype disagrees with the text.** It computed the digest "on the cafe template" (spec:891), which points at the resolved configuration; spec:177 says the committed version.
  - **Operator decisions.** ops:179 says unknown-source skills are "по умолчанию нельзя, включение осознанной настройкой". ops:167 and ops:180 say the librarian merges only on the operator's decision.
- **Derivation:**
  - **Template swap.**
    - The cafe project sets `"skills": "audited"` and composes `uses: ["cafe-ops", "bookkeeping"]` by name (cafe-project:5, :18).
    - An orchestrator installs `curator-playbook-cafe` at a newer audited tag. Alternatively, it installs another audited package whose orchestrator skill carries a template named `cafe-ops`, in a higher-precedence scope.
    - The resolved `tools` bindings, the roles' `needs.require`, the event signer groups and the workflows change, while `Playbook.json` does not.
    - Read as spec:177 words it, only T1's enumerated workflow changes wait for a person. A rebound tool command, a dropped reviewer requirement or a new signer group take effect as soon as the lock changes.
  - **Delegated installs.** The same happens under a delegation of `installs` (spec:291), which contradicts spec:277.
  - **Audit identity.** Nothing says whether an audit covers the exact package identity the lock records. A newer, unaudited release of an audited skill name may therefore pass.
  - **Hand copies.** Without Curator, an agent writes a skill with a `data/playbook.json` named `cafe-ops` into `.claude/skills`, and §8.4 resolves it with a warning only. That is an unknown-source skill admitted by default.
- **Resolution:**
  - Define the authority digest over the resolved configuration: the project file, plus each resolved template's authority-bearing content and its package identity. A template change then waits like a project change.
  - State that installing a template-carrying skill, or a new version of one, changes the process, and correct spec:741 accordingly.
  - Tie "audited" to the exact identity the lock records.
  - Treat hand-copied role skills and templates (§8.4) as skills from unknown sources: the resolver refuses them unless the operator's confirmed `skills` setting admits them. Say how this applies to the `.roles` overlays that PC1 and PC2 still read.

#### T3. The roster and key enrollments decide who counts as a person, and no confirmation covers them

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:176: "A commit is enough … a pull request is never required".
  - spec:179: "the kernel also verifies the signature of the commit it read against the roster at that revision".
  - spec:254: "A pattern (`op:*@acme`) is resolved against the waggle roster at the pinned revision. A quorum (§6.4) counts distinct principals present in that roster."
  - waggle:608 and :611 (new in A1): a companion key's "enrollment record carries `user_verification = "app-enforced"`", and its signatures meet `required`.
- **Conflicts with:**
  - waggle:63: the roster is committed in the project, and "Adding a teammate is a reviewed pull request".
  - waggle:197: "Enrollment evidence and its verification result are stored next to the roster (`.waggle/keys/<principal>/<fingerprint>.json`)".
  - ops:178: "не завязываться на git-хостинги". A pull-request rule for the roster ties it to hosting, and a project without hosting has no pull requests.
  - spec:177: the authority digest covers neither the roster, nor the revocation list, nor the enrollment records.
- **Derivation:**
  1. The scrum project binds `operators` to the pattern `op:*@acme` (scrum-project:9).
  2. An orchestrator commits two things: a roster line `op:bot@acme` for a key it holds, and an enrollment record claiming `attested` and `app-enforced`. It signs the commit with that key.
  3. spec:179 verifies the commit against the roster at that same revision, which now lists the key. The authority digest does not change.
  4. From CM2, `op:bot@acme` belongs to `operators` and counts as a distinct principal toward a quorum. If the verifier reads the stored "verification result" instead of re-checking the evidence, the key also meets `user_verified: required`.
  5. Adding a second key for `op:alice@acme` works the same way, without touching `people`.
- **Resolution:**
  - Put the roster, the revocation list and the enrollment records at the pinned revision into the authority digest.
  - Verify a commit that changes the roster against the roster of its parent revision.
  - Require verifiers to re-check enrollment evidence against the vendor roots, never trusting a stored result alone. A1 §19.5 then states which evidence proves `app-enforced`.
  - In waggle §3, replace "a reviewed pull request" with a commit plus a person's confirmation.

#### T4. What the kernel runs while a change is pending is undefined

- **Type:** internal · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:177: "Until then the kernel keeps applying the last confirmed authority sections … A change to the other sections … takes effect on commit".
  - spec:178: "A change that depends on a pending authority change — a state owned by a role whose requirements are not yet confirmed, a transition that names a tool not yet confirmed — waits with it."
- **Conflicts with:**
  - **The split runs through one object.** A role's `needs.require` and `on_unmet` are authority-bearing, while its `skill` and `prefer` take effect on commit (spec:177).
  - **Transitions do not name tools.** States owned by tool roles do (spec:233).
  - **Nothing validates the mix.** §10 validates "the resolved result" (spec:621). Nothing validates the hybrid of confirmed and committed sections, and nothing says what the kernel runs when that hybrid is invalid.
  - **Two digests.** Approvals and grants bind "the semantic digest of the resolved configuration in force" (spec:311, :461), while spec:175 records the digest of the committed file. Under a pending change the two differ.
- **Derivation:**
  1. In the confirmed version C0, the reviewer requires `not_same_publisher_as: "developer"`.
  2. Commit C1 renames `developer` to `implementer` in `roles` and in every workflow, and updates the reviewer's requirement accordingly. The requirement change is pending.
  3. The hybrid takes C1's roles and owners with C0's requirement, which names a role that no longer exists. Either §10 refuses the hybrid, or every review is unmet and `ask` fires until someone confirms.
  4. Whether the rename itself "depends on" the pending change turns on whether a new role without requirements changes the digest, which the spec does not state. If it does, the rename waits, and the hybrid combines C0's workflows with C1's roles, where `developer` has no skill.
  5. Similarly, removing a field that a confirmed delegation ceiling reads leaves a hybrid that §10 refuses (spec:883), although each version validates on its own.
- **Resolution:**
  - Either make the whole version the unit of waiting: while the head's authority digest is unconfirmed, the kernel runs the last version whose authority digest was confirmed, all sections included, and later commits wait with it until a person confirms or the change is reverted.
  - Or define "depends on" as a closure over names (roles, tools, fields, states, groups, slots), validate the hybrid, and keep the last applied version when it fails.
  - Either way, say which digest approvals and grants bind while a change is pending.

#### T5. Who confirms, and where a confirmation is verified, is unstated, so one board can run two processes

- **Type:** internal · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:174: "One board therefore has one process in force at a time".
  - spec:177: the authority sections take effect "once a person has confirmed the digest", and the kernel "notifies the operators".
  - spec:138 and spec:153: a confirmation is a signature by a key "registered" once, on the machine or at pairing.
- **Conflicts with:**
  - Goal 2 (spec:37): "The same file works for every teammate". Boards are shared: every teammate counts the same committed elements (spec:517), and the scrum demo binds `op:*@acme`.
  - spec:140: before CM2, no second operator's confirmation "can be produced and verified".
  - spec:314 and spec:527: before CM2, budgets and tick stay on a single host for exactly this reason.
- **Derivation:**
  1. Bob's orchestrator commits a wider `policy.data.models`.
  2. Alice confirms it on her machine, where her key is registered.
  3. Bob's kernel cannot verify her record, so it keeps C0, while Alice's kernel applies C1. One board now runs two processes.
  4. If kernels accept any recorded confirmation instead, a registration has no form that another machine can verify before CM2, so a key an agent registered cannot be told apart.
  5. Who may confirm is not stated. Could carol, an accountant, confirm a change to the managers' delegation policy? Could a person outside `people`?
- **Resolution:**
  - Name the confirmer, for example a principal of `people`. The author may propose that as the reading of "a person" (otherwise §5, Q4).
  - Before CM2, either confirm authority changes only on single-host boards, as budgets and tick already require, or state that each machine runs what its own operator confirmed and amend spec:174 for that period.
  - From CM2, make the confirmation a signed board act by a `people` principal, verified against the roster at the pinned revision and recorded on the board.

#### T6. Before the app exists, a headless node confirms through a key that does not show what it signs

- **Type:** external-constraint · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:1010: "until it ships, every confirmation is made on the machine, and a headless node is confirmed over the operator's own terminal session to it, with the account password or a FIDO2 key". track:55 says the same.
  - spec:147: "on the machine" means LocalAuthentication on macOS, Windows Hello, polkit or PAM on Linux, or a FIDO2 key with user verification.
  - spec:142: "This stops an agent that cannot answer the authentication prompt."
- **Conflicts with:**
  - spec:272 and waggle:142: "what you see is what you sign".
  - ops:177: "касание на машине или наше приложение". The terminal-session path is the author's fill, and it is unmarked.
- **Derivation:**
  1. **macOS over SSH.** LocalAuthentication prompts in the node's GUI session and cannot prompt in an SSH session, and PAM is listed for Linux only. On a headless Mac, such as the always-on Mac mini (waggle:433), the FIDO2 route is the only one listed.
  2. **FIDO2 over a forwarded agent.** A FIDO2 key reaches a remote node through a forwarded `ssh-agent`, and every process of the operator's account on the node can use that socket while the session lasts.
  3. **No display.** The key cannot show what it signs, so the PIN and the touch approve whichever request reaches it first.
  4. **The attack.** An agent sends its own confirmation request when the operator runs `curator models confirm`, or simply relies on a reflexive touch. It obtains a valid confirmation record over its own entries, while the operator's terminal rendered a different request.
  5. **Board acts too.** waggle:611 ("until it exists, board acts are signed on the machine") has the same gap for board acts on headless nodes.
- **Resolution:**
  - Before the app, confirm a headless node from the operator's workstation. A client command fetches the pending entries or sections over SSH, renders them there, signs with a locally attached key that needs user verification, and returns only the signature. The node verifies it against the key registered for that operator.
  - Never use the confirmation key through a forwarded agent.
  - Alternatively, list PAM through a privileged helper for macOS as well.
  - State the rule in §2.3 and waggle §19.5, not only in §15.

#### T7. The monthly review does not bind, and removing an open-ended answer leaves its exceptions in force

- **Type:** decision-conflict · **Severity:** major
- **Claim:**
  - spec:833: "An answer the operator leaves unanswered stays in force, is shown on the board as unreviewed, and is listed again." §24 (spec:1291) and decisions:144 record this without marking it as the author's.
  - spec:833 and spec:116: the review lists "open-ended" answers, meaning `until = "open"`.
  - spec:115 and spec:831: an applied answer "takes the role's approval scope and expiry … unless `until` sets an end date or says `open`".
- **Conflicts with:**
  - ops:172: "Бессрочные ответы на ask — разрешены; плюс периодический (раз в месяц) пересмотр выданных бессрочных ответов с подтверждением." The review with confirmation is the condition on which the operator allowed open-ended answers. An answer that keeps applying without confirmation makes the confirmation optional.
  - spec:829: exceptions are "scoped … and time-limited".
  - spec:316, and the principle behind S4: when authority ends, its unconsumed acts stop counting.
- **Derivation:**
  1. **Ignored reviews.** An operator who ignores the notices keeps every open-ended answer indefinitely.
  2. **Far end dates.** An answer with `until = "2099-12-31"` is not open-ended by syntax, so it is never reviewed, yet it relaxes the requirement as long as an `open` one does.
  3. **Orphaned exceptions.** With `open`, the exception an application creates takes neither the role's scope nor its expiry. dev-cycle's reviewer (`scope: { ancestor: "epic" }`, `expires: "P30D"`, dev-cycle:21) then gets exceptions with no end and no stated scope. Removing the answer at review stops new applications, but the exceptions already created keep relaxing independence in their scope indefinitely.
- **Resolution:**
  - An answer that is not re-confirmed within one period after its review stops applying until it is re-confirmed. That is the aligned reading of "с подтверждением". If the author keeps "stays in force", mark it as the author's and ask the operator (§5, Q1).
  - Review every answer whose end lies beyond the next review, not only `open` ones.
  - An exception created from a standing answer always takes the role's scope and expiry, or the §9 defaults; `until` bounds only when the answer applies.
  - Removing an answer, or letting it lapse, ends the exceptions it created; a run already working finishes.

#### T8. The first confirmation and the unconfirmed state are only partly defined

- **Type:** assumption · **Severity:** minor
- **Claim:**
  - spec:177: "A project's first version is confirmed when the project is created; until a first confirmation exists, the kernel runs no tool, honours no delegation and no relaxation, and reports the project as unconfirmed."
  - spec:180: "Before the first apply it reads the file as it is and warns that its authority policy is a guardrail only."
- **Derivation:**
  - **Who confirms at creation.** The spec does not say. The main loop and the process designer create processes (ops:167, :180; oq:49), and read passively, "is confirmed when the project is created" lets an agent that creates a project confirm its authority.
  - **The unconfirmed state.** It names tools, delegations and relaxations only. An unconfirmed file's `policy.enable.schedules`, `auto_spawn` and `skills`, and its `services`, `people`, `policy.data` and `policy.signatures`, have no stated behaviour. An unconfirmed project may therefore spawn runs, fire schedules and let agents install skills.
  - **Projects without git.** Before the first apply, spec:180 reads the file as it is, while spec:177 says that until a first confirmation nothing runs. The two describe the same period differently.
  - **Existing boards at PC1.** A board running the built-in default is neither created nor confirmed, and §14 lists no difference for the new notice. A legacy `task-board.config.json` has its ceilings read "as project admission" from the working tree (spec:129, :998), so for one release it widens admission without any confirmation.
  - **The default branch without hosting.** It has no definition. In a local-only repository, it is whatever `HEAD` or a configuration names, which an agent can change.
- **Resolution:**
  - A person confirms the first version, by the §2.3 methods. A project an agent creates stays unconfirmed until then.
  - An unconfirmed project runs the file-less baseline for every authority section: no tools, schedules, automatic spawns, delegations, relaxations, standing answers, agent installs or signed events.
  - Creating a project without git performs its first apply.
  - The built-in default needs no confirmation, because it grants nothing.
  - §14 lists the legacy admission read as an exception to row 26 for its one release.
  - The board records its default branch at creation, and changing it is authority-bearing.

#### T9. Pairing the companion app lacks the invariants that keep an agent out

- **Type:** authority · **Severity:** minor
- **Claim:**
  - spec:150: "Only someone who sees that terminal can pair, and pairing another phone later needs a confirmation from an already paired one or on the machine."
  - spec:271 and waggle:608: the app's key "is enrolled with attestation".
- **Derivation:**
  - **The QR code.** An agent that runs the initialization itself, or that reads a hosted session's transcript, sees the QR code.
  - **Attestation.** §2.3 does not require the node to verify the app key's attestation at pairing. Only §4.2 mentions attestation, and only for meeting `required`.
  - **Re-initialization.** Nothing says that re-initializing a node needs a confirmation.
  - **The biometric gate.** It is enforced by the app. The phone's OS could enforce it on the key itself (a Secure Enclave key whose access control requires biometry), which would not rest on the app alone.
- **Resolution:** state the invariants for the design that §17.3 item 11 leaves open:
  - the node verifies the app key's attestation at every pairing, and refuses a key without it;
  - initializing or re-initializing a node that already has a registered key, and every later pairing, needs a confirmation from a paired app or on the machine;
  - pairing refuses to run in an agent session;
  - the QR secret is single-use and short-lived, and never enters a hosted session's transcript or log;
  - the signing key's access control, rather than the app alone, requires biometry.
